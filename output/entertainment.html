Shigeru Miyamoto Proclaims November 1 is Pikmin Day, Pikmin 4 Demo Coming To Switch 2 Soon

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

This morning, Nintendo shared some updates related to the Pikmin franchise and has officially claimed November 1 as Pikmin Day, starting today and moving forward presumably forever.

 

Aside from the announcement of Pikmin Day as a general idea, Pikmin creator Shigeru Miyamoto appeared on camera to remind everyone that Pikmin 4 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy is coming November 12. A free demo for the game, which will include one standard level and two Dandori Academy levels, will be available on November 2.

The Switch 2 version of the game (you can read Game Informer’s Pikmin 4 review here) will have improved visuals and take advantage of the Switch 2’s mic to let you say things like “Rush” and “Go to work” to Oatchi. The Dandori Academy side of the game is essentially time trials you can practice, and then on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, you will have one chance to post your best time. You can read more about the Switch 2 version of the game here.

Miyamoto also shared that the mobile game, Pikmin Bloom, is also getting a fairly substantial update soon with secret caves. In Bloom, players will be able to find maps that reveal caves that you can send your Pikmin into to battle and find treasure. You can also find maps for Bloom by playing Pikmin 4. Caves will be coming to Pikmin Bloom later this year.

The Pikmin Day presentation also reminded viewers that every Pikmin game is available on Switch and Switch 2, though no announcements or hints were made implying that Pikmin 1 and 2 would be coming to the Nintendo Switch Online subscription service, which includes GameCube games on Switch 2. Those games can be purchased individually on the eShop.

Finally, Miyamoto brought actress Fuka Koshiba on the presentation (best known for voicing Kiki in Kiki’s Delivery Service) to share her appreciation for the Pikmin franchise (she likes to knit Pikmin hats that she shares on social media) and introduce a Pikmin short film. In the short, a Pikmin interacts with a toad, but I won’t spoil what happens. Miyamoto promised more shorts are on the way and in the future, they will be found in the Nintendo Today app.

Unfortunately, the stream concluded without a single reference to 2017's Hey! Pikmin. I wouldn't expect it to get a port to Switch 2 anytime soon.

Nintendo Reveals Surprise Pikmin Day Direct

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Nintendo has revealed a surprise Nintendo Direct for Pikmin Day because it has apparently decided that's a thing now.

Instead of spooky game announcements or horror movie memes, the first thing video game fans woke up to on Halloween 2026 was Mario creator Shigeru Miyamoto's face. That's because Nintendo has declared November 1 as Pikmin Day, and it's got a brand-new Direct to celebrate the occasion.

Yes, really. None of this is a joke. There's a little more than nine minutes of announcements to go over. Although there's no Pikmin 5 reveal or anything else too groundbreaking here, Miyamoto did take the time to reveal new information about Pikmin 4 while promising that, "going forward, I want people to know more about Pikmin."

So, to start, why is the first of November Pikmin Day? Nintendo's miniaturized franchise launched in Japan in October 2001 before coming to North America in December, so there's no tie to a release date like you might have guessed. Instead, Miyamoto explained that November 1 is Pikmin Day because there were originally only three Pikmin, which, when standing next to each other, kinda looks like "11.1."

Pikmin 4 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy

You gotta give 'em credit for creativity, as Pikmin Day is probably one of the last things anyone expected to see today. Still, it served as a nice opportunity to offer an update on Pikmin 4 – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy ahead of its release November 12, 2026. Players will be able to use the new consoles built-in microphone to Oatchi, controlling them by using commands like 'rush' and 'go to work.'

The Dandori Academy mode, meanwhile, comes with new challenges, including exams that take place on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, that give players timed tasks and an opportunity to earn medals. A demo for the Switch 2 version of Pikimin 4 will be available starting November 2, with save data available to transfer to the full launch. It offers access to the Sun-Speckled Terrace opening area, as well as two Dandori Academy stages.

Pikmin Bloom

Nintendo first brought its augmented-reality game, Pikmin Bloom, to smartphone devices in 2021, but five years on, and it's still got more to give. As shown in a teaser video featuring a group of the colorful plant-like creatures battling a Bulborb, an update scheduled to arrive this winter will add a new mechanic called Secret Caves. These battle-focused exploration events allow players to take their crew into caves to battle creatures and earn rewards.

Before a cave is explored, players must collect a cave map, which feature information about a cave and can be found on things like Postcards. Then, Pikmin are sent into the cave to search for treasures, with some of the items highlighted during the Direct including a rubber duck, a sword, a cookie, and a chef hat. Should a battle ensue, you'll command your army by sending them in to attack when the moment is right, or retreat if things get too hairy. Pikmin 4's Dandori Academy challenges also give players an opportunity to collect maps by taking exams.

Avatar: Seven Havens Showrunners Address Your Biggest Finale Burning Questions

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Full spoilers follow for Avatar: Seven Havens Season 1!

The first Book of Avatar: Seven Havens is, er, in the books with this week’s finale, “The Root Chakra.” And while the final four episodes answered a number of big questions, they raised a ton more — not just about what to expect in Season 2 of the Paramount+ animated series, but Seasons 3, 4, and perhaps beyond. It’s a good thing, then, that we have show co-creator Michael Dante DiMartino and showrunners Sehaj Sethi and Ethan Spaulding on hand to answer all your burning questions.

…Or at least, answer what they can. While we got to see Pavi (Saheli Khan) step up as the Avatar in the finale, unlock her Root Chakra, and head off on a classic Avatar journey to the Floating Isles next season, Nisha (Aishu Devan) — surprisingly — was not part of that unlocking and is staying (for now) in the Haven of Ellora. Not only that, but we also saw Nisha was at least in part the cause of the Fear Anomaly that’s been attacking Ellora all season long, activated when her parents abandoned her; as a side effect, she accidentally killed her parents in a rock slide.

But there are still the questions of why and how Avatar Korra caused a Cataclysm 225 years earlier, how there are twin Avatars now, and many more mysteries and surprises to explore. So let’s get into it and see what DiMartino, Sethi, and Spaulding can answer about the future of the Avatar franchise.

More: Avatar: Seven Havens Season 1 Finale Review and Recap

Remembering Eric Bauza

Before we get to that, though, earlier this week the world of animation suffered a shocking loss with the passing of actor Eric Bauza at age 46. Bauza voiced multiple characters in this season of Seven Havens, and given the pace of animation, we’ll likely hear his voice for several seasons to come. But his passing does leave a huge gap in the world of Avatar.

“Eric meant a lot, obviously, to the show,” Spaulding said. “He's all over Seven Havens, and his contribution to animation in general is so prevalent. He's going to leave a big hole.” Sethi continued, “He worked on this show, but we all have history with him beyond this show. He's worked on so many things. I joked all the time that Bauza was twenty percent of the population of Seven Havens. We used him because he was so brilliant. He could act against himself. He could do young, old, any voice you could think of… Every single time he was in that booth, he was an absolute delight… He was just the kindest person… It's just the biggest loss.”

But Bauza’s contributions stretch beyond Seven Havens.

“He was actually in Avatar: The Last Airbender,” DiMartino recalled. “That was when he was just getting started in his voice-acting career. I think he's like a guard or two in the Boiling Rock. So the fact that he's been part of this universe for so long… I knew he was a big deal, [but] hearing all the stories from people over the past week, just learning more about him and the impact he's had on the community and everyone he's worked with is, it's just very sad. And [we] wish his family the best.”

Pavi And Nisha… Separated?

To get into the show itself, it’s a surprise that Pavi heads off on the classic Avatar journey at the end of the season alongside airbender Jae (Major Curda) while Nisha stays behind in Ellora. That, of course, was always part of the plan.

“From the very beginning, we all wanted this to be a really organic story about two sisters,” Sethi said. “Yes, there's a lot of world-building. There's so much that's going on. But at the end of the day, they are two young girls who fight and have differing opinions. Now that we've spent a whole Book, a whole season, seeing them grow together… What is it going to be like now when they're apart? How are they going to grow apart from one another into their respective roles? That was really interesting to me.”

Added Spaulding, “Also, getting the characters out into the world so the audience can see… other locations, it's a cool thing to have the characters split up at some point in the storyline for the world-building.”

Welcome to the Floating Isles

Part of that world-building is the next Haven Pavi is headed towards: The Floating Isles. We’re told she senses trouble there, and it seems likely that’s the location of the next Chakra she needs to unlock. So what can they tell us about the place?

"We try to make every Haven they're going to visit its own mini-culture [with] different customs and everything."

Not much. “That's Haven Number Two, and Pavi will be visiting there,” DiMartino confirmed. Calling it a “more chill place compared to Ellora,” which was greeted by knowing laughter from Spauling and Sethi, DiMartino continued, “It's definitely a different vibe culturally. We try to make every Haven they're going to visit its own mini-culture [with] different customs and everything. So it'll be fun to start seeing more of the world as we get out there.”

There Will Be More Than One Haven Per Season

Given Book 1 was entirely in Ellora, one might venture that Book 2 will be entirely in the Floating Isles. This would mean that either the show is going seven seasons or we’re going to go a little faster than we did in Season 1; turns out, it’s the latter.

“No, not exactly,” DiMartino said when asked about the ‘one Haven per season’ idea. “We had to split it up a little differently, but we'll get through all the Seven Havens by the end of this series for sure.”

Beyond that, they confirmed that the four seasons of the show is the entire series. “That’s the full story,” DiMartino said. Added Sethi, “When Mike, Bryan [Konietzko], and I started working on it, we always had an end goal in mind — a lighthouse for us to work the story towards — and that's always been the case throughout the series.”

Is Pavi the “Real” Avatar?

Apologies in advance, because the answer to this one won’t be satisfying, but we did ask about the revelation that Pavi’s chakras are messed up and she is able to unlock the Root Chakra alone but not with Nisha. So is repairing the Cataclysm solely up to Pavi? Is she the “real” Avatar, and is Nisha a side effect or something else?

“We’ll see,” Sethi said enigmatically. “You gotta keep watching, man. I promise all of those questions are answered.”

Hey, we tried!

Going Dark

One of the darkest moments in Avatar history is when it’s revealed that not only did Nisha’s parents abandon her, but in the process of summoning the Fear Anomaly, she accidentally killed them in a rock slide. While Sethi confirmed that there’s more to this story to come, it’s still a horrifying duo of revelations that skew way darker than fans were likely expecting.

“I don't want to speak for Mike or Ethan, but when we started the series, I always had a feeling this is going to be darker,” Sethi said. ”We were really tackling some difficult and complex situations. The amount of power these girls wield… I always was interested in the idea of putting very young characters in very mature positions. I thought there was something really interesting there, especially from a storytelling point of view. We're really forcing the girls to grow up in a lot of ways, and so for me that was just an interesting place to go as a storyteller. For the show and for where we are taking the story, it made sense to start dividing the girls on not just a philosophical level but an emotional level, and this is the biggest emotional divide you can give two characters. So to me, I really wanted to explore that in the upcoming season.”

Added DiMartino, “In the early development that Bryan and I did, we had [the] idea of this tragic backstory that happened with Nisha, the family, and Pavi. That's how she lost her leg. So the core of that ended up being what's in the show. We always knew that was going to be a big part of the mystery of Book One and a big reveal, and like you said, it's a dark moment. But it's totally necessary for their characters and the story, and so it all ties together. And yeah, there's more that we will dig into around that story in upcoming seasons.”

Korra Constellations

During a climactic moment in the finale when Pavi unlocks the Root Chakra, she also sees a vision of four past Avatars: Kyoshi, Aang, Roku, and finally, Korra. While Spaulding explained this is a nod to the constellation versions of the Avatars seen previously in both The Last Airbender and Legend of Korra, there seems to be a little more to it. And no, it’s not Pavi reconnecting with the Avatar Cycle after Korra broke the connection towards the end of Legend of Korra.

“It’s something else,” said DiMartino. “She has not reconnected. It can't happen that easily. So she does have a connection with Korra, but the other stuff's more of a vision.”

Added Sethi, “That's something we really had to work [on showing] visually that the connection is not complete. It was not just, oh, let's show Korra for a second or something. It was like, hey, she's sort of faded, right? She's not completely there. We wanted it to not just be so easy as, yup, now Korra can talk to you. That's something that still needs to progress in the series.”

Fans did notice in earlier episodes that both Korra and Asami are wearing betrothal necklaces in a “mover,” while in a vision of Korra where she’s tearing down Republic City, she is not. So how worried do we need to be about Asami?

“Asami is definitely dead by our show, which I think is a fair thing to say.” Sethi said. “The main thing to know from that is that there is most likely a time jump. But as to their relationship, you do learn more about that in Season 2.”

More LGBTQ+ Relationships in Seven Havens

One huge leap forward for the franchise is the amount of LGBTQ+ representation on screen. The final episode of Legend of Korra pretty famously had some issues in showing Korra and Asami getting together back in 2014. In 2026? Not only are the duo clearly together (and maybe betrothed), but we’ve also followed a canonically gay couple in Jae and Datu (Topher Ngu), and at least one other female-female pairing has shown up on screen.

“I definitely think that TV [and] even the kids’ animation landscape has definitely changed,” DiMartino reflected. “Whereas on Korra, we ran into some… pushback about what we could and couldn't show, this time around everyone was super supportive and it's more about the storytelling. What is the best way to show these relationships? It's nice to see, and we will see more of what Korra and Asami's relationship was like in the past.”

Return of the Afflicted

Despite Pavi unlocking the Root Chakra, things aren’t quite settled in Ellora, particularly since Chancellor Kayoko (Jennifer Sun Bell) is still clearly possessed by the Fear Anomaly as the season ends. So what’s going on here? Is the Root Chakra still blocked?

“The afflicted stay afflicted until a certain point,” Spaulding explained. “So there's still something that the Avatar has to do.”

Added Sethi, “Yeah, their journey is not finished with just releasing that energy. There's more that they'll need to do.”

We’re Not Getting a Huge Time Jump (Probably [Yet])

Fans at New York Comic Con noticed some designs of Pavi and Nisha that seemed to depict their characters aged up significantly, but as Sethi clarified, “that was concept art. So whether there's a time jump, who's to say, but that was not a time jump piece of art. That was just concept art.”

Other than that, though, time will be progressing forward as it tends to do. “Within the span of a series, there's time in between episodes,” Spaulding explained. “It's not very big, but you could say they're traveling for weeks or months. It’s similar to Last Airbender.”

“I give those amazing young ladies so much credit that they really learned how to grow their performance as the characters got more complex."

“Yeah, now that they're on the road, [you] gotta account for the travel time in certain ways,” DiMartino added. “Time passes, but it's not like we're jumping forward years or anything.”

Instead, they’re letting the growing maturity of the characters come from subtle animation changes, like a loosening of Pavi’s hair in the finale and the vocal performances of the actresses.

“I give so much credit to Saheli, who plays Pavi, and Aishu, who plays Nisha,” Sethi said. “I give those amazing young ladies so much credit that they really learned how to grow their performance as the characters got more complex. They did a great job, and shout out to our amazing voice director Kristi Reed, who helped them with that.”

Seven Havens: Fury Road

One of the fun parts of Seven Havens has been picking out post-apocalypse tropes hidden between the Avatar easter eggs. From a Mad Max-style introduction to two other Havens, to a zombie movie-esque Afflicted infestation, the series has embraced the setting in a big way. And while Sethi conceded they don’t have a list of tropes (or movies) they’re trying to hit, it is very much baked into the premise.

“The thing that I always found interesting when Mike and Bryan brought me this idea was that it's almost like post-post-apocalypse,” Sethi said. “They've created a new city, right? They've essentially already done the work of rebuilding, and now it's almost like, oh, is there another impending apocalypse coming? So that was the interesting thing of [doing] some post-apocalypse stuff, but it can't feel like Scavengers Reign because they've rebuilt. There are new places. We tried to weave it in where it really made logical sense with where the story was going and where the world was.”

"The thing that I always found interesting when Mike and Bryan brought me this idea was that it's almost like post-post-apocalypse.”

Added DiMartino, “When you get into Book Two, though, and now that they're traveling, you're going to see more of the Harshlands and stuff. So that gives you more of the post-apocalyptic vibe, and why people aren't living out in these areas.”

Seven Havens Gets Political (or Not)

Despite the multi-year schedule of creating animation, Seven Havens has felt laudably and eerily relevant to our world today. Turns of phrase in the finale like “We can’t just hide from the scary bubble” and “freedom of choice is all I’ve ever wanted” work on multiple levels. So how does a staff aiming for a hopefully timeless children’s animated series also reflect what we’re feeling right now in 2026?

“In a lot of ways, we were trying to really uphold an ethos that a lot of the Avatar world has always upheld,” Sethi explained. “I don't know if it's us getting political or everything that is relevant about humanity just being political at this point. To be truly honest, these were things that we always wanted to talk about, and we always felt they were timeless. We just happen to be in a time where they are incredibly relevant now.”

Added DiMartino, “All the Avatar stuff we've done has some political bent to it, but we're trying to address themes that are pervasive throughout history, not just talking about the current political climate. These are things that societies and people have struggled with throughout civilization’s history.”

“For me, especially, the idea of fear of the other, freedom of choice,” Sethi continued. “These are things that absolutely matter and will always matter. Like Mike was saying, the fact that they matter right now… Honestly, it's kind of cool that people can watch our show and feel a relevance now. But even 20 years from now, I think they'll still feel that relevance.”

The Hardest Part of Writing Seven Havens

Now that the secret of the show is out — that each of the Seven Havens does indeed align with one of the Seven Chakras — it gives the story a very clear, mission-based structure. But what it also does is somewhat dictate where Pavi and Nisha need to go emotionally over the course of the four seasons. So how do the writers balance honoring the emotional truth of those character’s journeys versus “well, we’ve gotta hit this chakra next.”

“Dude,” Sethi said, groaning. “That was the hardest part of the show. Truly, it was a massive undertaking. We did everything we could to make sure that their journeys would align with the world but would also align with them personally as organic characters. This is the hardest show I've ever worked on, and in the room, that was something that we went over and over. We probably tried like 17 different storylines because we wanted to make sure that the one we hit matched the characters as well as this overarching concept. So yeah, the best thing I can say is: It was really hard.”

Added DiMartino, “It's nice to have a structure [for] what we're going to be dealing with thematically. But then you have to honor that and also the characters we have. The thing I was excited about doing, now that we can talk about all the chakras stuff, is that the Guru was one of the people's favorite episodes from Avatar: The Last Airbender. But we had to cruise through the chakras real fast, so when we were revisiting that idea and the series, it was like, oh, this would be cool that we could structure the entire series and really explore these themes and make it not so easy to to unlock all of them. [We made] that the main journey of what Pavi has to go on.”

The Future of the Avatar Franchise From Seven Havens to Wave of Chaos and Beyond

There’s a lot coming in the future of Avatar, including three more seasons of Seven Havens, a new series titled Avatar Kuruk: Wave Of Chaos, and potentially more TBA. So let’s check in on what’s even further down the road than Season 2.

On Where They Are in Seven Havens: “We've been working on this stuff for several years now, so we are deep into Books Three and Four,” DiMartino said. “And yeah, it'll be a little bit before they come out, but rest assured, it’s all being worked on.”

Will Seven Havens and Wave of Chaos Overlap on the Release Schedule? “That is beyond my scope of control,” DiMartino said, laughing. “So it'll depend on what Paramount Plus wants to do, I guess, or whatever the streaming app happens to be at that time. That's just getting started. It might be a bit before we have episodes ready for that.”

Will There Be Further Avatar Shows? How About Movies? “Potentially,” DiMartino said. “I mean, we've got ideas for other Avatar things. We can come up with ideas all day, but we need the higher-ups to greenlight stuff.”

You hear that, Paramount? Start clicking that green light button, because we need more Avatar in our lives!

Death Stranding creator Hideo Kojima showed up for a cameo role in a new animated short from legendary rock outfit My Chemical Romance.

That's probably not the first sentence you expected to read heading into the Halloween weekend, but considering Kojima is known for his love of music, movies, games, and everything in between, it may not be too surprising. Still, fans of his work as a creative powerhouse were probably shocked to see him appear in the credits for the band's new animated short film, Prelude to The End.

The seven-and-a-half-minute project, which arrived as a surprise drop yesterday evening, is a stop-motion, dark fairytale based on director Lauren Tsai's The Dying World. Serving as a vehicle for a 20th anniversary music video for My Chemical Romance's "The End." – the opening track for the New Jersey group's seminal rock record, The Black Parade – the video also enlisted Kojima for a quick voice role.

Just as main character, Astrid, retreats to her room to parse through a few missed calls, we get Kojima's My Chemical Romance music video debut. He's credited simply as The Teacher, and can be heard scolding Astrid about late work in her third voicemail. You can see the full text below.

"Astrid your Japanese homework is late again. This is unacceptab-" Kojima's The Teacher can be heard saying before the recording is cut off.

Kojima is far from a stranger when it comes to cameos, but he's been especially busy in this department lately. The Metal Gear Solid series, which Kojima created and orchestrated for years, features a few moments where he broke the fourth wall for a cameo. He also appeared in CD Projekt Red's Cyberpunk 2077 for a role that he recently reprised in Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2.

Death Note Is Doing An Unexpected Collab And I’m Already Obsessed

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

As both a woman and complete and utter nerd, nothing makes me giddier than when those two core facets of my personality come together in an inspired way. Such is the case of Mooncat's Death Note collaboration, which sees the manga-turned-anime-turned-live-action-film adapted into something a bit different: nail polish.

After a week of teases, the Mooncat x Death Note collection is now live. The collab features nine different polishes, each inspired by a specific character or moment in the dark-yet-beloved series, and marks the first time Death Note has collaborated with a beauty brand.

If like me you are a die-hard Mooncat fan, you already know that their polishes are anything but simple, and this collection is no exception. The collab features nail lacquers in shimmer, matte, glitter, thermal, metallic, and magnetic finishes, all in a gorgeous range of moody colors. The descriptions for each polish are as follows:

  • Sunset Over Tokyo: A deep blue laquer filled with yellow shimmer.
  • God of the New World: A charcoal black to burgundy themral lacquer with a subtle transition. It transitions from a shade of burgundy red when cold to charcoal black when warm.
God of the New World Sunset Over Tokyo
  • Sugarfuel Brain Calories: A semi-sheer lacquer jam-packed with blue shimmer, cand-colored flakies, star-shaped flitter, holographic glitter, and reflective glitter.
  • Mourning Rain: A matte grey lacquer filled with green shimmer.
  • Misa Misa: A purple-pink magnetic lacquer that reveals a black base when magnetized.
  • Rem's Requiem: A light pink-to-lavender multichrome magnetic lacquer that reveals a charcoal base filled with silver metallic flakies and reflective glitter when magnetized.
Rem's Requiem Misa Misa
  • Ryuk Was Bored: An icy silver-blue magnetic lacquer that reveals a navy blue base with reflective glitter when magnetized.
  • Gods of Death Love Apples: A matte lacquer filled with red shimmer.
  • Shinigami Realm: A slate grey to silver thermal lacquer filled with icy shimmer that transitions from a shade of slate grey when cold to silver when warm.

Each polish runs between $17-18, and a magnetic wand--which assists in the application of magnetic polishes and making cat-eye effects--can be added to your cart for free with any purchase.

Fans looking to grab the whole collection can also order the limited-edition collector's box set, which is housed in a matte black container meant to resemble the series' eponymous Death Note. The collector's box set retails for $139--about $20 less than if you were to buy each polish individually--and comes with an exclusive sticker set and magnetic wand.

As someone who has been following the collaboration's rollout via Instagram, I am particularly impressed by the level of thought that went into the collection. According to Mooncat's creator, Michelle Lin, the set is "a love letter to a story whose artistic legacy has left an indelible mark on her creative vision." Lin has spent the past week or so regularly uploading short reels explaining her thought process behind each shade.

God of the New World, for example, is meant to represent the show's main character Light, who portrays himself as a straight-laced, normal teenager but hides a dark, bloodthirsty side; this duality is exemplified by thermal polish's transition from charcoal to crimson depending upon temperature. Sugarfuel Brain Calories, on the other hand, is far more light-hearted, and pokes fun at fan-favorite character L's insatiable sweet tooth.

The Legend of Zelda Series Producer is Worried About Ocarina of Time Remake Ahead of Release

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Despite The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time being widely considered to be one of the greatest games of all time, series producer Eiji Aonuma has admitted that he is still quite worried about the release of the upcoming remake. In an interview with AFP (via Japan Today), Aonuma spoke about the title’s release.

“I’m all nervous about releasing the remake of ‘Ocarina’ because I don’t know how it’s going to turn out; I can’t stop thinking about it,” he said. While many might find Aonuma’s thoughts on the matter puzzling, his worries shouldn’t come as a surprise to anyone since the upcoming title is a remake of a game that was originally released all the way back in 1998.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has always been well-regarded for defining many of the aspects that would go on to become core parts of the entire franchise’s identity. This ranged from the Z-targeting lock-on system to the interplay between unique items and dungeons, puzzle design, and even how the core combat works. Not only have these aspects been further refined in the almost three decades since the original release, but more fans who got into the Zelda franchise in more recent times might even find them puzzling.

The last mainline entries in the series have been The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom. Both of these titles had an emphasis on the open world, player traversal, and a gameplay sandbox that could allow players to toy around with the special tools given to them early in the games. However, Ocarina of Time, while still featuring a relatively open design, is a lot more linear than what modern fans might be used to.

Interestingly, the fact that the Ocarina of Time remake might bring in a younger audience plays a major role in Aonuma’s worries. “It’s thanks to these young people, by thinking about how to adapt the game in the best possible way, how to make it fun once you have the controls in your hands, that this remake came into being.”

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time’s remake was officially unveiled in June with a trailer giving us our first look at the game’s depiction of Hyrule in modern visual fidelity. The title is also set to include a few other changes over the original, such as the addition of a dedicated jump button, expansions to various zones like Kakariko Village and Goron City, a streamlined inventory system that allows for single-button item swapping, and even some entirely new additions, like boss rematches and the Hall of Fallen Foes.

The remake is making its way to the Nintendo Switch 2 on November 5th. The title faces quite a bit of stiff competition, since it is coming out just two weeks before the juggernaut that is Rockstar’s Grand Theft Auto 6. Avatar: Seven Havens Season 1 Finale Review and Recap

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Full spoilers follow for Avatar: Seven Havens Season 1, Episodes 10-13!

One thing that’s abundantly clear in the final four episodes of Avatar: Seven Havens “Book 1: Survival” is that between all the mysteries, theorizing, and big plot questions – how are there two Avatars? What did Korra do to cause the Cataclysm? What happened to Pavi (Saheli Khan) and Nisha’s (Aishu Devan) parents? – the far more important question is, and has always been, how this will impact the relationship between the twins? And to answer that, the finale is a heartbreaker, featuring one of the darkest, most upsetting endings in the Avatar franchise, while still somehow providing hope, heart, and above all, humor.

The Long View

That’s a lot to tackle, and to be clear we do still get movement on the overarching mysteries of the series that should take us all the way through Book 4. That includes the reveal of what’s needed to heal the world… While fans sort of guessed what was going on, and there is likely much more to come, it turns out that the Seven Chakras that coincide with Seven Havens are directly tied to Pavi. Specifically, as the Faceless Queen Agam (Sakina Jaffrey) discovers during a spiritual alignment in Episode 11, “Battle of the Ruins,” Pavi’s chakras are all messed up. And once she’s able to face her fear of being revealed as the Avatar in the finale, “The Root Chakra,” she unlocks – guess what – her Root Chakra.

Read Last Week's Review:

Avatar: Seven Havens Episodes 7-9 Recap and Review

Both we, the audience, and Nisha are surprised that it’s Pavi who can unlock these alone; it was completely reasonable to speculate that the only way of stopping the fear literally bubbling under the Haven of Ellora was through Nisha and Pavi working together. Instead, the additional shocker is that somehow Nisha summoned that fear, killing her and Pavi’s parents in the process. Pavi was protected from the resulting rock slide by some sort of green energy, which took her leg… And Nisha, while able to hold back an energy dome about to consume the Haven in Episode 12, “The Dome,” is nowhere near as strong as Pavi, who single-handedly dispels the fear and begins to reconnect with past Avatars, as well as charting her path to her next destination, The Floating Isles (or Wilds – there are no closed captions on the screeners provided by Paramount).

(Side-note: there’s a ton of lore we could get into here, but we’ll save it for the section below.)

But in between all that, these episodes are really about two things: Pavi and Nisha, and how we react to fear. On the former, the show has managed to craft a powerful sisterly bond over the course of 13 episodes that feels real… Pavi is gutted by the revelation that Nisha killed their parents, but she’s far more upset that Nisha lied to her about it. And we’re left in a place where Pavi is finally owning up to her destiny, but seems harsher and more closed off (despite unlocking the Chakra) than the goofy weirdo who befriended rocks and bugs in the initial episodes. Meanwhile, Nisha is being cheered by the crowd in Ellora at the end, a flip on the fear of the Avatar that dogged her every move. But she’s just seen Pavi display more power than she ever has, and that clearly alarms her. They haven’t exactly changed positions, but it’s clear this resolution isn’t as simple as “hooray, fear cured!”

That gets to the second part, which we’ve been harping on all season: fear. The show hasn’t necessarily been subtle about how it’s reflecting our current world (or at least the world of several years ago when they were writing, developing, and animating this), but when a character says “We can’t just hide from this scary bubble” or “Freedom of choice is all I’ve ever wanted” they’re basically putting it out there what metaphorical levels the show is working on. And what works about it is that the series still functions as a rollicking adventure show and a sweet all-ages animated show while still being very blunt about where it leans politically.

And that’s what the best of entertainment should be doing, right? Commenting on our real world while also working as, you know, entertainment. Seven Havens has absolutely nailed that balance over 13 episodes. Not only that, but these final four address a number of fan “concerns” that have bubbled up. One of them has been about the “weak” bending, which one could venture comes from the fact that bending is disappearing from the world (as stated multiple times) and which we now know at least in part comes from these seven Chakras being blocked. But also, Pavi taking on the fear energy dome in the finale rocked. No pun intended on earthbending, but saving up your big bending stunts for the finale makes sense in terms of the build of a season.

The other question about the show has been why Pavi and Nisha are nine years old, with concerns raised that they’re trying to age down the Avatar series even further than has already happened. While one could point out here that it’s a children’s cartoon on Nickelodeon, and apologies this isn’t catering to you, an adult who watched the original series 20 years ago, this finale cemented that not only is this show going crazy dark and upsetting – with Pavi and Nisha’s parents abandoning Nisha, then dying in a rockslide caused by Nisha – but at the very least Pavi is starting to grow up by the end of the episode. Her hair is different, and Khan does a masterful job of maturing her voice. There’s been speculation about a time jump going into further seasons, and given the epic journey nature of the show (as also set up in the finale) that seems likely… They won’t be nine forever.

The Episodes Close Up

Okay, before we get too much farther, let’s recap the specific episodes (briefly), though they all work nicely together as a four-part finale. First up is Episode 10, “Ghosts and Spirits.” The thrust here is that Chancellor Kayoko (Jennifer Sun Bell) has been driven mad by the fear anomaly, and has the whole city under martial law. While Pavi deals with the metaphorical ghosts of her parents after discovering her childhood home, Nisha encounters the first spirit we’ve seen on the show, which leads her to the place we later discover she freaked out at and killed her parents. And in the midst of this, Agam discovers the duo and leads them to her hideout.

Then in Episode 11, “Battle of the Ruins,” Kayoko makes the exceedingly poor decision to redirect the bubbling pit of fear energy under Ellora to the hideout of the Faceless. We also discover definitively that Jae (Major Curda) never betrayed the Order of the White Lotus; he was just trying to not get arrested so he can free them later. We also get info on the Chakras from Agam, and they end up fighting the bubbling fear energy together, barely escaping in time.

Episode 12, “The Dome,” heads back to Ellora as Nisha solo tries to stop the energy, now in the form of (yep) a dome, while Pavi holds it back. Kayoko isn’t having any of it, and breaks Nisha’s hold, releasing the dome, which consumes our twins… Leading to a vision of their mother. Also, Jae and his boyfriend Datu (Topher Ngo) break up, which is a real bummer for ‘shippers but lets Jae be free to travel in the next episode.

And then we’ve got the finale, Episode 13, “The Root Chakra,” which we’ve covered a chunk of already. To touch on a few other points/where we leave folks, Pavi leaves for the next Haven with Jae by her side, while Nisha stays in Ellora with Karthik (Akshay Khanna). Kayoko is still possessed by the fear entity, which seems… interesting. Agam, meanwhile, seems to stay in Ellora, while she names Zi (Dianne Doan) the Faceless Queen, and the rest of the Faceless presumably head to Republic Forest, as planned.

Avatar Easter Eggs, References, and Connections

Before we get to the Easter eggs and references, it’s important to recognize the untimely, awful passing of voice actor Eric Bauza at age 46 on October 25. While Bauza didn’t voice any of the main roles on the series, he was, as always, a utility player. In these final four episodes alone he’s credited with Jin-Woo, Townsperson, Elloran Citizens, Kosal, Pich, Faceless, Faceless Bandits, Faceless Kids, Firebender, Guard #2, Benders, Guards, and Ellorans – just to give you an idea of how important this legend was to the series. And not only that, but he also stood out as Pudding Guy in the previous chunk of episodes, a delightful addition that seemed to be the heir to The Last Airbender’s Cabbage Merchant. What they’ll do with that role in subsequent seasons is TBD, but Bauza leaves a gap in the world of Avatar that can’t readily be filled.

Not to make an abrupt turn, but let’s talk about those blocked chakras. This was first introduced in The Last Airbender and explored when Aang was injured by Azula, and was unable to access the Avatar State. Here, while Pavi goes HAM on that red energy dome in the finale, she does not enter the Avatar State at all, likely because her Chakras are blocked. But we do get a strange red glow around her, and that greenish glow that protected her from Nisha’s rock slide in the flashback, which seems to be something new.

Speaking of something new, when Pavi does finally unlock her Root Chakra, she sees constellation versions of the previous Avatars Kyoshi, Roku, Aang, and finally Korra (aka, the four Avatars before Pavi and Nisha). Without getting too deep in the weeds here, at the end of Legend of Korra, thanks to a big fight between Raava and Vaatu, the spirits of order and chaos, Korra’s connection to the Avatar Cycle was severed, then begun anew. So whatever Pavi is seeing in this Rainbow Road/constellation deal is something different (or, since Korra went on many adventures after the final season of Legend of Korra, perhaps she experienced something different first).

As for Easter eggs, these episodes are remarkably light by comparison to the rest of the season, but we do have a few. Jae proudly declares “I will always be Team Avatar” in Episode 11, giving us our first glimpse of this show’s, well, Team Avatar – something we’ve gotten in both The Last Airbender and The Legend of Korra.

In Episode 12, Pavi makes an absolutely ridiculous mud mask for Nisha to disguise her – which works, as an Elloran refers to her as “old man.” This is just part of a long tradition of Team Avatar having the absolute worst disguises that, somehow, beyond all reason, always actually disguise them.

And one blink and you’ll miss it Easter egg, as everyone flees the energy dome in Ellora at the beginning of Episode 13, sees a citizen quickly walk in front of the camera holding – what else – an armful of cabbages.
