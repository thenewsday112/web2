Summary

  • Chaotic new take on hockey out now on Xbox Series X|S.
  • Brutal fights, ridiculous takedowns and riveting action within a unique narrative.
  • How we imagined a game inspired by childhood hockey playing and storytelling.

What happens when five Finnish developers take the hockey they grew up playing on frozen ponds and turn it into a story about the greatest underground hockey player who ever lived?

When I think about my childhood, some of my best memories aren’t from school, holidays or birthday parties.

They’re from the ice.

Growing up in Finland, I spent enormous amounts of time on frozen outdoor rinks and ponds, playing hockey with friends. You didn’t need to organize it weeks in advance or check everyone’s calendar. You just went to the rink. Games happened. Friendships happened. Some of the people I met through hockey back then are still my friends today.

There was something special about those games. The rules could be flexible, teams could change, and somebody would inevitably try something ridiculous. That feeling became one of the starting points for Puckoff.

When we created Puckoff: Reloaded, we wanted to bring some of that feeling back. Many of the game’s rinks are frozen outdoor ponds inspired by the places where we grew up playing.

Then we asked ourselves a simple question: What if winning didn’t mean scoring more goals?

A Different Kind of Hockey

In most hockey games, you know what you’re supposed to do: Get the puck. Score goals. Win the game. In Puckoff: Reloaded, there are a few more options. You can score. Set up a teammate. Make the perfect pass. Or you can knock someone out.

And nobody in this world seems particularly bothered by that last option.

That’s the world we wanted to create: a story-driven hockey game where fighting isn’t a random side activity, but part of the sport and its strange underground culture. I’ve played a lot of the big hockey games over the years. They’re great at what they do, but I always found myself thinking: “Why can’t I just do that?”

You know the feeling. An opponent does something you don’t like, and you want to give them a little… extra attention. But the game tells you no. That’s when I realized one of the things I love about games. They let us experiment, cause chaos and do things we wouldn’t necessarily do in real life. So, I thought: what if a hockey game actually embraced that? That’s how Puckoff: Reloaded began.

In our version of hockey, scoring goals is still useful. It’s just not the only way to win.

We’re Not Just Making a Hockey Game

I’ve always loved storytelling, and for years I’ve had a particular dream: What would it be like if a big hockey game actually had a real story? Not just a career mode where you play a season and upgrade your player. I mean a proper story. Characters you care about. Mysteries. Rivalries. Friendships. A reason to wonder what happens next. That’s what we wanted Puckoff: Reloaded to be.

Between matches, the game uses a visual-novel-inspired presentation to tell a larger story. The hockey games aren’t disconnected challenges. They’re part of a journey. And that journey is about finding a legend.

The Search for Dominator

At the heart of Puckoff: Reloaded is a hockey legend named Dominator. He’s been playing underground hockey matches for decades, and nobody really knows how he’s managed to keep going for so long. What everyone does know is that he has never lost a game.

For the player character, Dominator isn’t just another hockey player. He’s the reason to keep playing. The dream is simple: find Dominator and eventually play alongside him. Getting there means meeting the people who inhabit this underground hockey world and proving you’re good enough to belong in it. Eventually, that leads to the Cage.

Welcome to the Cage

The biggest fights in Puckoff: Reloaded don’t happen in a traditional hockey arena. They happen underground.

The Cage is where some of the game’s most dangerous opponents are waiting. These boss matches are different from ordinary games: you’re not simply facing another team and trying to score more goals. You’re facing a character with their own identity, abilities and way of playing. And if you’re going to get closer to Dominator, you’ll have to get through them.

Our Dream is Bigger Than This Game

I’m proud of what our five-person team has created, but I also see Puckoff: Reloaded as the beginning of something. My dream has always been to make story-driven hockey games with the scope and production values of the big-budget games. I want hockey games where you remember the characters as much as the matches. Stories that make you care who wins. I want a game where you can spend an evening playing hockey with your friends, laughing when somebody gets knocked out—and then come back the next day because you genuinely want to know what happens to the characters.

We’re not a huge studio. We’re five people in Finland making the game we wanted to play. For me, that’s what Puckoff has always been about. It started with the frozen ponds where some of my best childhood memories were made. Now we’re taking those memories, adding a ridiculous amount of fighting, creating a hockey legend who hasn’t lost in decades, and turning the whole thing into a story.

We just have to find Dominator first.

Virtua Fighter Crossroads Showcases Lionel Rafale in New Trailer

Following teases by Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio from earlier this month about a new announcement for Virtua Fighter Crossroads, the studio has now released a new trailer giving us a look at one of the title’s roster members – a returning Lionel Rafale. The trailer, which you can check out below, features 2 minutes of gameplay footage alongside some story moments.

Lionel has been described by RGG Studio as “a mysterious young martial artist” who shows up in the city of Vilasapara. Further in the description, the studio has said that he is “a highly skilled fighter capable of outclassing Cielo in a street fight,” and went on to say that “he is a force to be reckoned with.” However, his story remains a mystery, much like his true reasons for showing up in Vilasapara.

Around half of the trailer is dedicated to showcasing Lionel’s moveset, with which he is able to put his opponent on the back foot thanks to a variety of knee strikes, punches, and kicks. Lionel also has a few grapples with which he can trip his opponent, leaving them on the ground and open to more attacks. He ultimately finishes the fight by throwing his enemy against a wall and doing a high kick assisted with a single-palm handstand.

Virtua Fighter Crossroads was officially unveiled as the official title for RGG’s New Virtua Fighter Project back in June. At the time, the studio released a trailer showcasing some of the game’s visuals and its story. The core setting of Virtua Fighter Crossroads – Vilasapara – will see many fighters arriving for a massive competition. However, there are also other problems to face, including the Chinese mafia that is seemingly on the run.

In a further showcase, the studio confirmed that Virtua Fighter Crossroads won’t simply be a fighting game. While it will still feature the franchise’s traditional VS Mode, the core of the title will be an “open sandbox RPG” with an emphasis on player choice. Considering the development team’s history, it is easy to assume that this game might follow a similar path to the Yakuza/Like a Dragon and Judgment franchises in offering players a dense world to explore paired with an emotional story.

June also saw the release of a trailer showcasing the Bakunawa Killer. The character is seemingly sneaking around the city of Vilasapara and waiting for the perfect chance to take out the fighters who have decided not to participate in the tournament that will form the core of the game’s story. The Bakunawa Killer’s identity remains unknown, however, and his trailer even had Cielo and Pai Chan chasing after him. However, before his true identity could be revealed, the trailer simply comes to an end.

As for his fighting style, the Bakunawa Killer will seemingly make use of a wuxia-inspired moveset with high-impact strikes. He will likely also be capable of some intense acrobatics that will end up giving him an edge when it comes to positioning and setting up crossover attacks.

Virtua Fighter Crossroads doesn’t yet have a release date aside from a general 2027 window, and RGG Studio hasn’t even confirmed its target platforms so far.

Destiny Fans Shouldn’t Expect Any Announcement at The Game Awards, Says Head of Product

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

While there might be plenty of excitement regarding the future of the Destiny franchise following Bungie’s announcement that it will slowly bring back vaulted content into Destiny 2, the company has now said that fans shouldn’t expect any major announcement at the upcoming The Game Awards. In a new blog post, the studio spoke about its plans for the future, and the fact that it still has plenty of things it needs to figure out.

Head of product Josh Deane has confirmed that Bungie is currently in the pre-production phase of its long-term content unvaulting plans. The studio is specifically targeting content from campaigns, strikes, and raids to be brought back, along with the Menagerie and all of its activities. After this, the studio is looking at other things it could bring along as stretch goals, like more PvP maps and updates to raids and their associated weapons. And finally, Bungie might look at how it can improve the New Light experience, and we could even see things like seasonal story missions and their associated hubs return.

As for the timeline, Deane has said that the current timeline indicates that pre-production and the proof-of-concept stage will end by February, following which the full-blown production will kick off. All in all, the studio expects it to be completed in around 12 months from the first announcement.

“I think before we close it’s important to know that this is a true reset moment for Bungie, Marathon, and Destiny. We also know time isn’t our friend; we’re playing from behind with the cards we have and I’m sure we’ll have to take more punches and make some hard decisions along the way,” Deane explained. After discussing how the company has to work on its ongoing projects with different constraints and the need to allocate resources to them in appropriate ways, he went on to say that fans shouldn’t expect to see the Destiny logo randomly show up during The Game Awards.

“Knowing we are early in incubating our future and actively working on projects to bridge us to that new home, don’t expect us to make a Destiny announcement at the TGA’s this year, or to randomly drop a logo that sets the world on fire right away.”

In the meantime, Deane has also spoken about what it means for Bungie to be “not done with Destiny”. There are seemingly three things planned for the game: a “Celebration” beyond Moment of Triumph, “Legacy”, which might indicate plans to bring Destiny 1 content over to Destiny 2, and a “Redacted” thing. The last one wasn’t really detailed, with Deane simply offering a tease by saying, “Launch Ventrilo Message: ‘We’re back’”. Each of the three Destiny-related projects will get their own progress bars, with “Celebration” and “Legacy” getting theirs in February.

On the subject of “Redacted”, Destiny 2’s communications manager Dylan Gafner said that the “[I] always love when we use that.” He went on to reassert the fact that Bungie wants to be more transparent with its fans, and the progress bars for its projects are meant to be promises “we intend to keep”.

Yaga Episode 3 Recap and Review

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Warning: This review contains full spoilers for Yaga Season 1, Episode 3. Episodes 1-3 are now streaming on AMC+ in the US and Crave in Canada, with new episodes releasing on Fridays at midnight PT.

Yaga Episode 3 wastes no time diving right back into this eerie, witchy tale, while answering some of our biggest questions after last week’s debut.

We pick up right where Episode 2’s cliffhanger left off – on the beach with Logan’s body – prior to getting confirmation from the police that, yes, it is in fact Logan’s lifeless body (not Henry’s) and that blood was found near a window open in his bedroom. There’s a moment of confusion for the viewer as Rapp sees Logan’s eyes jump open, and we see that they are ice blue – like Henry’s.

By now, we know Rapp has been hallucinating more frequently and this is possibly either from the drugs from the night before (because he honestly looks like he’s still high) or something dark and ominous that’s happening to him.

We see a pattern in relation to “time” in Yaga. This is confirmed when Carson recounts what happened the previous night at Henry’s party where we learn that “a few hours” had passed between seeing Logan disappear into his room upstairs, and Carson finding Rapp on the beach. Originally, it seemed like some time had passed, but nothing substantial.

Time Blips

In Episode 1, there was a time blip between text messages Rapp received seemingly one after another in the bar scene while speaking with Katherine. His text from Carson comes in at 8:11pm, and another text – only seconds later – comes in from Angel at 9:41pm. From what we see as viewers, time has stayed the same in the environment of where he is, but something is clearly controlling time in relation to him. (I noticed this after the publishing of the premiere review, and I blame missing these subtle clues on being only human and not a witch myself.)

As we continue on with more unexplained moments in this story, Rapp tries to put a move on Carson in his hotel room, which she rejects. He then goes to lie down on his bed and falls asleep almost too quickly – Carson comes and kneels down next to the bed and we see the clock turn to 3:33 (not the first time we’ve seen this either), while intense, whispering voices begin to take over as she leans in to almost kiss him. Rapp gently opens his eyes and sees Pam, Henry’s ex-girlfriend, seductively pulling a white feather out of his mouth.

He suddenly jolts out of his actual sleep and awakens some time later in the day. We also notice a small circle tattoo on Carson’s wrist before they head over to forensics, which seems to be placed in the shot intentionally for us to refer back to later…

Going to the mortuary, we see Logan’s corpse – well, most of it. In the episode’s biggest WTF moment, we learn Logan’s penis was severed from his body and he was castrated. It turns out he was also stabbed with an ancient knife made out of Obsidian and human bone – which Katherine later classifies as an Aztec ceremonial midwife sacrificial knife, connected to deities with symbols representing sexual desire and fertility. All signs so far point to Logan being murdered, and Carson and the police on staff are searching for more evidence, especially as this new case potentially relates to Henry.

We see Carson walk up to a small church to meet Logan’s mom, Alice (played by Sarah Strange). The dark, moody shots and heavy music come together to create an emotionally beautiful scene of grief between the two women. They sit on a bench in the cemetery together and more is revealed about Logan that we hadn’t known before. That includes Logan changing his hair and working out constantly – like he was trying to become Henry himself, perhaps.

That thing getting bigger and thicker and darker and pulling him further and further away from God, and closer and closer to the Devil.” Carson asks about Logan’s dad, but Alice dismisses the questions.

While Carson is with Alice, we see Rapp jogging on the beach. Pam happens to be running in front of him. He tries to catch up to her and, as he does, she all of a sudden splits into three versions of herself, distorted in Rapp’s view. Before Carson and Rapp decide to head back over to Henry’s house to check for more leads, Carson inquires if Henry and Logan were possibly in a relationship, giving Rapp unintentional fuel to speculate on Carson’s sexuality as well, which she avoids divulging.

As the duo begin surveying Logan’s room, you can see the pile of bloodied sheets next to the window, and they begin slowly uncovering more and more clues as they go along. In Celtic mythology it is said to represent sacred femininity and the Triple Goddess archetype: the Maiden, the Mother, and the Crone. Baba Yaga in Slavic folklore is known as the “Crone,” so we’ll have to wait and see how that relates (if at all) as the series continues.

Carson and Rapp also notice the chicken foot hanging from Logan’s lampshade, previously in Henry’s room – and we can now pretty safely assume it’s from the chicken he and Henry were rumored to have sacrificed.

A desk with a tape recorder and an archive of dozens upon dozens of cassette tapes are stacked on shelves.

As they dig in further, Carson and Rapp find out there’s a recording from the previous night in Henry’s bedroom – from when Logan was having sex with the woman wearing the angel wings. Nocturne Episodes 1 & 2 Recap and Review

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Spoilers follow for Nocturne Episodes 1 and 2, which are available on Apple TV now.

Apple TV’s new thriller series, Nocturne, aims to draw audiences in with just the right balance of the familiar and the unexpected. The show asks what happens when a boy thought to be the victim of a serial killer returns alive, and follows the criminal mastermind as he maneuvers law enforcement during their race to save any other victims who may still be out there. Over the course of these first two episodes, viewers are left with questions — a mix of plot-driven intrigue, interest in character development, and a touch of side-eye around potential logic gaps or plot conveniences.

Nocturne is based on Lars Kepler’s crime novels (The Sandman and Lazarus), and adapted by Rowan Joffe and John Hlavin for TV. These debut episodes lay the groundwork for convicted killer Jurek Walter’s unfolding scheme to free himself (he is astonishingly well played by Stephen Graham), set against multiple law enforcement officers’ attempts to a) stop him from doing more harm and b) find any of the still-living victims. Jurek was convicted of a series of brutal killings, which devastated a small town. He managed to get a jury to buy into an insanity defense, or we must presume he did, as he’s in a psychiatric hospital rather than a prison. (Side note: how he pulled that off is a nagging question, as nothing in his demeanor suggests an insanity defense would fly… If it’s simply that he’s a sociopath, wouldn’t that apply to all killers? Moving on.)

From a technical perspective, the show does a beautiful job of dropping us in this world. The opening shot of Episode 1, “Lazarus, Come Forth,” establishes a sense of foreboding, danger, and claustrophobia that will become a refrain throughout the series. We get the sense that the creature we are encountering is not unlike a lion in a cage. The camera moves down a cold, concrete hallway toward cells that feel (purposefully) hidden and forgotten — the shot is all the more suffocating because the dark walls of the cell entryways initially obscure the sides of the frame. This is our introduction to Jurek, and it’s notable that the series starts by putting us in the mindset of the killer. He feels trapped and eager to get out. For some yet-to-be-revealed purpose, his gritty, simultaneously menacing and soothing voice whispers to the patient next door, luring the man to seek freedom by any means necessary, even with violence (in this case, against himself). This is Jurek’s central arc across the first two episodes.

He is brilliant and adept at psychological warfare. He also seems to believe he’s entitled to his freedom, or at the very least that there is some legal loophole that will get his conviction overturned if only he can expose the lie that former homicide detective Jonah Lynn (Liev Schreiber) told to seal Jurek’s fate. Jurek taunts Jonah towards the close of the first episode with an offer of “the truth for the truth” when Jonah and Special Agent Quinn (Poorna Jagannathan, lately of Lanterns) visit him. In other words, Jurek will help them find more of his victims, specifically Felicia Frost — the missing sister of Michael, the boy who’s come back from the dead — if Jonah confesses to lying in court. This raises the stakes for everyone, including Agent Quinn, who’s already taken a risk by bringing a retired cop she doesn’t know this close to her case. If his old lie blows it up, that’s on her, with a missing girl’s life on the line.

While some crime dramas give us only as much information as the characters searching for answers have, this one puts the audience a step ahead. It’s an interesting choice, and may ultimately be a misdirect.

While some crime dramas give the audience only as much information as the characters searching for answers have, this one puts the audience a step ahead. It’s an interesting choice, and may ultimately be a misdirect. Here’s what appears to have happened: a hospital worker named Karl (Rory Culkin) is assigned to the team that oversees Jurek. Their first encounter, like others in the series, is crafted with impressive tension: Karl searches for a missing weapon beneath Jurek’s metal bed, gets caught by his pant leg, and can’t free himself before Jurek comes to from a sedative — the trapped animal motif plays throughout. Before the scene is over, Jurek has pressed a note into Karl’s pocket, urging him to smuggle it out to his lawyer (more on the questions this raises in a bit).

The note Jurek hands Karl seems to set everything in motion: the strong implication is that, lawyer or no lawyer, it contained Jurek’s instruction to an accomplice to free Michael. Jonah is actively drawn back into the case when Michael turns up alive, as the children’s mother, Ida Frost (Chrissy Metz), and the residents of the town trust him more than anyone else in law enforcement. Also advocating for his participation is FBI agent Saga Bauer (Zazie Beetz), his goddaughter, the child of his deceased partner, and someone he effectively raised. (She eventually goes undercover as a patient in Jurek’s secured ward in order to ascertain who is leaking messages and endangering lives.)

The reality is, Jonah has clearly never given up on finding the remaining missing persons associated with the case: as Agent Bauer discovers via his giant murder board, he’s been methodically searching an enormous grid, single-handedly, for years. The case is deeply personal for both of them. Jonah carries guilt for having quit the force after the (seemingly unrelated?) accidental death of his wife and child. And Jurek (or rather his accomplice) was responsible for kidnapping Bauer’s stepmother, Rebecca. It’s also implied that Bauer’s father committed suicide after his wife’s disappearance. Through Agent Quinn’s vocal skepticism, the show addresses the inappropriateness of having either Jonah or Bauer involved in the investigation, given their personal connections to Jurek. The story excuses the conflict of interest (not to mention that Jonah is no longer an officer of the law) by pointing to their familiarity with the case and the ticking clock on saving Felicia. The dynamics and interwoven tragedies all tied to Jurek stretch credulity, but the production value and strength of the performances are enough to keep the viewer engaged through a good deal of character, plot, and relationship table-setting. At its core, this is a battle of three savants with real stakes: two on the victims’ side, deploying their individual skills to best a mysterious, murderous genius. That brings us to the tightrope the series walks.

When crime dramas lean on preternatural intellect, whether it’s Sherlock Holmes or his foil, Moriarty, they run the risk of reading as a lazy sort of magic trick, where the detective or killer effortlessly “knows things” they’d need to in order to keep the story moving. Indeed, Jonah’s near-instant recognition of where Michael was being held, and Jurek’s Derren Brown-level ability to read people (pegging Karl’s guitar playing and Jonah’s partner’s violent alcoholism), open the door to the kind of conveniences that could lose a modern audience as the series unfolds. It already causes one to wonder: if Jurek is in possession of that kind of mental acuity, why did he get caught while his partner went free? Fortunately, Graham, who stunned the world with Adolescence last year, is deeply compelling, allowing us to give ourselves over to his performance.

That isn’t to undercut the potent talent of the rest of the cast, or the visual storytelling. The show is beautifully shot, with well-crafted production design. It immerses us in a rustic exterior world that vacillates between gray and worn, and comfortingly familiar. The forest landscapes are striking, but dense and dangerous in a way that mirrors the cold, stifling confines of the hospital Jurek is housed in.

Schreiber is delivering a grounded, understated portrayal of a grieving man who is just a bit too tired to fully fight against terrible ideas (Bauer’s undercover operation) and far too invested to stop himself from digging, with or without permission. One moment that stands out is when Agent Quinn tells Jonah she’s sorry for the loss of his wife and child; he nods, and unbearable pain flashes in his eyes for an instant before he moves on. Beetz is convincing and deeply affecting as a chameleon-like agent willing to harm herself (physically or otherwise) to close a case. It’s clear that this particular operation may drive her to a breaking point for multiple reasons: Jurek’s uncanny ability to read others and play on their weaknesses, her own stepmother’s disappearance (which he is connected to), and, not least of all, that it taps into the trauma of a woman who was the child of a mentally ill mother and addict father. That fear comes through as she prepares to enter the ward, right after visiting her mother. Beetz gives the sense that Bauer is unsure whether she’s crossing a line she won’t be able to come back from, where fact and fiction are too close. Bill Camp is also endearing as Chief Ellison, particularly as he talks about what the Frost kids meant to the town; he looks vulnerable, like a man who knows he is out over his skis, but wishes he wasn’t.

It took a moment for me to recognize Culkin as the compassionate nurse, Karl. Physically, his hair is doing a great deal to support the transformation, but this is also a side of the actor less seen on screen. There was one moment, when he first sees Jurek on the security cameras, in which I was left to wonder if he had some nefarious intent. But for the most part, he reads as a man of incredible kindness and skill with patients whom most of society writes off and exiles. That said, it’s odd that an employee who demonstrates an extraordinary amount of empathy is tapped for a job that puts him right in the crosshairs of a person known to exploit those instincts, and in a dangerous environment that requires a certain degree of toughness. Additionally, Karl, who is clearly driven by financial need (he accepts the new job the moment he’s informed that it comes with a raise and tells Jurek he can’t afford to lose it), inexplicably risks his career for a dangerous criminal he just met.

So, that leaves us with a couple of options: this is a piece of plotting that doesn’t logically track, or Karl isn’t who we think he is. If it’s the latter, perhaps Karl was “sent” into the hospital with purpose. It’s strangely convenient that Jurek’s day nurse went missing (and is likely dead), making room for Karl to take over. Theoretically, the patient who injures himself in the opening sequence could have gotten word to Jurek’s partner to get rid of the day nurse and make room for Karl. But how could Jurek have known that the exact right personality type for his purposes was working a few floors above him, let alone that he’d get the job? Was Karl a plant, then, and Jurek’s accomplice? That doesn’t square with their first encounter, where, with no witnesses, Jurek treats Karl as a stranger and insists he is still a human deserving care. And if someone else on staff was steering Karl, Jurek already has a partner on the inside, so why would he need Karl? Those are just a few of the questions that may or may not pay off at the close of the series. There are smaller dangling threads as well. For example, Jonah finds footprints both in the building where Michael was held and outside the missing day nurse’s home, so why in the world would he hold them back from the FBI? Ostensibly, he’d do anything to find Felicia, so that type of behavior simply doesn’t track. Ironically, the serial killer is doing a better job of asking for help and relying on a partner.

That said, the series works in no small part because Graham is simply impossible to look away from. He’s playing a killer who appears to have a (forgive the Dexter reference) code that makes sense to him. He says he “wants people to pay for their crimes.” Yes, that was in reference to Jonah’s “lie,” but the way he looks at the nurse who is rude to Karl reminds me of Hannibal Lecter’s deep disdain for bad manners. Graham’s depiction of Jurek is utterly magnetic and mercurial; when Jonah and Quinn visit, he breaks into a fit of giggles at the idea that she’s a “special” agent, as if the word itself is a joke, and moments later he’s commanding the room, telling them with understated menace that they’re only there because he brought them there. He really does seem to own them. The minute he’s off the screen, I crave more time with him.

This entirely serves the series, because it allows us to believe that others could fall under his spell, even Bauer, a smart, capable, stony FBI agent with oceans to lose. There is a deep irony that he’s maneuvering and clawing his way to freedom, given his brutal kill method of burying victims alive. He complains that he’s been in solitary, but his partner has kept a child in the dark so long that the boy went temporarily blind. There’s also a very small possibility that he is, in some way, innocent, and that he has been “made worse” by his circumstances. Either way, for those on both sides of the law, this is a story about freedom, loss, redemption, and revenge, and the first two episodes have given me enough to stick with it for now.

We’ve just launched Dune: Awakening on PlayStation 5, but we’re only getting started. We’re excited to bring two more of our open-world survival adventures to PlayStation 5 in early 2027.

Soar the skies in Aloft, then visit the unforgiving Exiled Lands in Conan Exiles Enhanced.

Aloft: Build a home among the clouds

Play Video

After six years of heartfelt development with a team that grew from a small group of friends to the Astrolabe of today, we are ecstatic to reveal that our cozy co-op sandbox survival game in the clouds, Aloft, makes its console debut with Funcom as a publisher on PS5 early 2027.

The PS5 version includes all the updates released during the game’s Early Access period on PC, as well as the 1.0 experience, bringing our complete vision of Aloft to life, filled with new content, stories, and so many improvements.

Turn floating islands into skyships, build a home among the clouds, and explore a colorful open world alone or with friends.

Since launching in Early Access, we have introduced five major content updates to Aloft, featuring new wildlife, puzzles, combat improvements, glider customization, expanded exploration, and much more. Discover lost knowledge, cure fungal corruption, care for your animals, brave hurricanes as you restore the ecosystem and uncover new adventures across the skies.

Launching the complete game on PS5 marks an exciting new chapter for us. Aloft has always been a passion project fueled by the unwavering support and love of our community. Reaching this milestone is incredibly exciting, as this will mark a new era for the game – we really hope you will be part of our flight.

Conan Exiles Enhanced: Survive the brutal, yet beautiful Exiled Lands

We are thrilled to announce that Conan Exiles Enhanced is coming to PS5. All players who own Conan Exiles on PlayStation will get the Enhanced version for PlayStation 5 for free.

Both PS4 and PS5 versions will be available to purchase. Buy one version and get the other for free. All your purchased Bazaar items and DLC will also carry over from PS4 to PS5 seamlessly.

The Enhanced edition brings improved environments, characters, lighting, and effects, along with a redesigned UI and performance and quality-of-life improvements.

Play Video

The update also makes exploring the Exiled Lands and the Isle of Siptah expansion a more seamless experience. Isle of Siptah remains a separate purchase.

The new Legacy of the Giant-Kings story arc will be included in the console launch of Conan Exiles Enhanced, bringing an exciting new narrative experience to console players from day one.

Players will be able to uncover the mysteries of the ancient Giant-Kings, explore their forgotten legacy, and embark on a journey filled with new challenges, discoveries, and rewards. Its inclusion ensures console players can experience this major story arc as part of the enhanced Conan Exiles experience at launch.

We’ll share release dates and more details for both games at a later date. Wishlist both games now at PlayStation Store.
