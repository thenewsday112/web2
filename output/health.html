Life-Sim Meets Otherworldly, Encroaching Horror in Village In The Shade

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Cozy life-sim games where you get to retreat to a rustic village for a relaxing, self-sufficient life on a farmstead are hardly in short supply--and, with so many stellar entries in this genre out there, a game following this formula needs a really good hook to stand out. Village in the Shade, a new release from NIS America on Switch, Switch 2, PlayStation 5, and PC, certainly looks the part of a cheerful, cozy farming game. But, as the phrase goes, looks can be deceiving--and, in this case, that deception runs deep. 

You can tell something is off right from the tutorial, which sees you chasing after an escaped chicken, only to discover something rather distressing. What happens next only leaves you with more questions and some highly unsettling imagery. Somehow, after all this, your player character emerges, stumbling into Kagatsu Village in urgent need of care. 

The Kagatsu villagers seem very attentive at first, but it quickly becomes clear that the people in charge aren't entirely altruistic. It feels like they see you less as a suffering victim and more as an engine to reap profits. You're thrown onto dirty farmland overrun with debris--which has been oh-so-kindly leased to you--and forced to sleep in a run-down shack that looks like it came from a match-3 mobile game ad. If you want to stay with the villagers, you're told, you will need to become one of them. And you'll have to work for it. Nobody will so much as speak to you until then. 

When you finally raise your first, scant batch of turnips--and offer them in a ritual sacrifice ceremony--you're made an official Kagatsu citizen. But then come the rules. The rules must be followed. They're strange. Arbitrary. Controlling. Almost cult-like. "Do not expose another person's secrets." "Do not search for what was lost." "Do not approach strangers." And, the one most commonly emphasized: "Do not go outside after 11 PM."

But the people here seem nice enough, now that they're actually talking to you. Rokkaku's a single parent who runs the local restaurant and cares deeply for his adopted niece Yoh. Hasumi might not have a medical license, but she does seem to care deeply about everyone's health. Sazanka runs the general store and wants to provide the tools for your success. Kisuke might be crass and grumpy, but you can see that the builder has a good heart underneath it all. They'll teach you how to fish, how to set up animal pens, how to hunt for wild game, and how to build a variety of tools. They all seem like a pleasant bunch, even if they can be standoffish at first. (Well, except that sleazeball Konno. Guy has "manipulative crypto bro" written all over him.)

And yet, it feels like everyone's hiding something. You catch glimpses of strange behavior, snippets of conversations likely not meant for your ears. There are noises at night. People knocking on your door at ungodly hours, screaming incoherently. Oh, no, it's just Yoh; she's lonely and wants to sleep with the baby chick she adopted that lives in your pen. No harm will come of that, right?

Eventually, circumstance forces you to break that most sacrosanct of rules, and you step out during midnight. It's here that you come face-to-face with otherworldly abominations, chasing you, obstructing your vision, making the haptics on your controller go absolutely wild. You've seen something--many things, actually--that shouldn't have been seen by humans. You suspected it, expected it, and yet it’s still no less disturbing.

You begin second-guessing the motivations for everything. Submitting your produce at a town gathering for a public evaluation contest seems innocuous enough, but it happens mere days after you've been asked to either tithe or submit to a blood-gathering "medical exam" for reasons unknown. You can't help but wonder if hunting some animals might actually be an act of mercy, given what you know is lurking out there. And there are all of those areas that are conveniently, conspicuously blocked off. Sure, you’re going to open them as you progress, but what kind of progress do you have to make? 

This is the reality of Kagatsu: a place that hides horrors beneath deceptively warm and inviting coziness. The graphics are beautifully drawn in a 2D, painterly style that's immediately appealing. The day-to-day life-sim gameplay is easy to pick up, and it's satisfying to build your farmstead enterprise from nothing. But even in its brightest moments--when you raise a bumper crop, finally craft that new setup that lets you make you make a new kind of foodstuff to sell, or help the townsfolk with their errands or give them thoughtful gifts--there's always that creeping, pitch-black shadow welling underneath. 

I've been enthralled by Village in the Shade since I picked it up. It weaves its themes of deception and unease into a routine, leaving you in a bizarre emotional state of feeling both comfortable and on-edge. The contrast between the familiar warmth of a life-sim game and a hideous, horrifying underbelly is enthralling, and I find myself both eagerly anticipating and dreading every new development in my cozy town life. If you’re looking for a different kind of life-sim--or a different kind of horror game--you’d do well to take up your tools and join the village.

Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls Details Phoenix Cyclops’s Skills and Alternate Form in New Video

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

With Phoenix Cyclops joining the roster of Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls as its first DLC character, Arc System Works has released a new Character Guide video, detailing his abilities. Check it out below.

It begins with Optic Blast, a simple beam attack that can be charged for more damage, bounce off the floor, and even evade projectiles. This is also one way to gain Solar Shards and trigger Dark Phoenix form, enhancing attacks with Surging Flame, letting you move in a specific direction after it connects (and takes some health). So when using other skills like Heat Wave, Tactical Strike, and Phoenix Dive, it opens up a lot of potential for mix-ups.

However, you need to decide carefully when to use it because Solar Shards can’t be garnered for the rest of the round after the transformation ends. Afterwards, Cyclops enters the Ashen State, possessing many of the same abilities as his default state.

As for his Ultimate Skill, Dark Ascension, it can change depending on these conditions. If used after collecting all the Solar Shards, he’ll transform into Dark Phoenix; without them, he’ll immediately attack and transition into the Ashen state. Use it when he’s already Dark Phoenix, and you’ll deal far more damage.

Phoenix Cyclops arrives on November 2nd for Year 1 Character and Stage Pass holders. He’s followed by Thor (Jane Foster), Venom and Jubilee.

Summary

  • Titan Quest II launches on XBOX Series X|S on January 19, 2027, and is available for pre-order starting today.
  • Players combine two of six Masteries into a hybrid class that perfectly matches their playstyle.
  • Both the Standard Edition ($59.99) and the Ultimate Edition ($79.99) include the Cosmic Turtle Companion and 96 hours of early access.

High above the clouds, the gods of Olympus watch in silence – for once, even they are powerless against what is coming. The fate of the world now rests in mortal hands. Your hands. But do you have what it takes to face a goddess? The THQ Nordic and Grimlore Games teams are thrilled to announce that Titan Quest II launches on XBOX Series X|S on January 19, 2027. And starting today, you can pre-order the game on XBOX.

Titan Quest II is the successor to the beloved original Titan Quest: an action RPG set in a reimagined ancient Greece, where you set out to stop Nemesis, the Goddess of Retribution, from corrupting the Threads of Fate. Since the game entered Early Access on PC in August 2025, we’ve worked hand in hand with our community. We read the feedback, ran the numbers, and added new regions, new Masteries, new enemies and bosses, crafting, multiplayer, tons of loot, and much more along the way. With the full release, the story reaches its conclusion, and the complete journey comes to XBOX.

So, what makes Titan Quest II stand out in a genre full of great action RPGs? It starts with the heart of the series: the Mastery system. Instead of locking yourself into a fixed class at the start of your adventure, you pick two Masteries and combine them into a hybrid class of your own. Titan Quest II launches with six Masteries – Earth, Storm, Warfare, Rogue, Forge, and Spirit – and each of them can be freely paired with any other. That’s 15 distinct combinations before you’ve even looked at your gear.

Earth commands fire and stone for devastating area damage. Storm calls down lightning and frost to control the battlefield. Warfare is all about raw physical might, while Rogue strikes from the shadows with poison and piercing blades. Forge, inspired by Hephaistos himself, reinforces your weapons and armor in the middle of a fight and deploys blade traps, sentry turrets, and ferociously fighting automatons. And Spirit lets you summon the dead and draw on your hero’s life force to fuel otherworldly spells. Want to set up blade traps crackling with lightning? Pair Forge with Storm. Rather send the dead ahead while you strike from the shadows? Spirit and Rogue have you covered.

And the customization doesn’t stop at the pairing. Every skill can be shaped with modifiers that do far more than add a few percent of damage – some change how a skill behaves entirely, like turning a sweeping strike into a channeled whirlwind. Attribute points in Might, Agility, Vigor, and Knowledge let you fine-tune your hero even further, and if you ever want to rethink your build from scratch, you can respec at any time (the gods charge a little gold for it, of course).

Did we mention that every hero can use whatever weapons and equipment they prefer? You can wear full plate armor while wielding a frost magic staff, or charge into battle clad in leather while swinging a two-handed axe. And an archer dressed in fine linen works perfectly well with any Mastery. The choice is truly yours.

The second thing that sets Titan Quest II apart is how it plays. Combat is deliberate rather than frantic. Enemies fight in factions and combine their abilities, so reading the battlefield, dodging at the right moment, and smart positioning matter just as much as raw damage. Even your health potions encourage you to stay in the fight: they refill as you deal damage to your enemies. IGN Germany summed up the Early Access version as “the ARPG for everyone who finds Diablo too hectic” – and we’ll happily take that. With Rituals, you also decide for yourself how hard the gods should test you, adjusting the challenge freely to your liking.

Loot matters, too. Items aren’t just bigger numbers; they serve a real purpose for your build, with plenty of equipment options for every class. Socket Charms and Relics into your gear, and use crafting to reroll, add, or upgrade affixes until every piece is exactly what your build needs.

All of this takes place in a beautiful, handcrafted world rooted in Greek myth. Venture across sun-drenched coasts, the Arkadian Plains, the untamed Wild Lands, and even Mount Olympus itself. Discover dark dungeons, uncover hidden groves where legendary monsters lurk, and stumble upon World Quests you can complete simply by finding them. Along the way, you’ll face creatures straight out of legend, from centaurs and gryphons to cyclopes, all set to a soundtrack that blends traditional Greek music with orchestral grandeur. And if you’d rather not take on Nemesis alone, you can explore Greece together with friends in cooperative multiplayer.

Ready to answer the call? Pre-orders for Titan Quest II open today, October 28, on XBOX Series X|S, with two editions to choose from:

  • Titan Quest II Standard Edition – $59.99 / €59.99: Includes the Cosmic Turtle Companion as a pre-order bonus and 96 hours of early access. Owners of the original Titan Quest on XBOX also receive a 10% loyalty discount.
  • Titan Quest II Ultimate Edition – $79.99 / €79.99: Includes the Cosmic Turtle Companion as a pre-order bonus and 96 hours of early access, plus the original Titan Quest and its expansions Ragnarök and Atlantis – playable immediately upon pre-order.

With 96 hours of early access, you can begin your journey as early as January 15, 2027. And if you go for the Ultimate Edition, the original Titan Quest and both expansions are yours right away – the perfect way to warm up for the sequel (or to finally find out what happened in Atlantis).
